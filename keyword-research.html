Start with the audience, then the keywords.

Keyword research connects what your business offers with the language people use when they need it. A useful keyword plan explains the audience, the purpose of a search, and the type of page that could answer it. A long list of phrases without that context is difficult to turn into useful work.

For a Zikap research project, the starting point is your services or products, target location and language, and existing pages. These inputs keep the recommendations relevant to the business rather than simply chasing the broadest terms.

How the research is organized

Audience & seedsCollect starting topics from your offering, customer questions, and existing content.
Intent reviewDistinguish searches for learning, comparing options, finding a brand, or taking an action.
Search landscapeReview relevant results and competing content to understand what a useful answer should provide.
Topic groupingCombine related phrases when one page can answer the same underlying need.
Page mappingAssign topic groups to existing pages or identify a justified need for a new page.

If search-volume or difficulty estimates are included, the report should name the data provider, location, and collection date. Those values are estimates, not guaranteed traffic, and no live keyword database is connected to this website.

From three searches to a useful page plan

Consider a hypothetical garden shop. Someone searching “how to grow herbs indoors” may need a practical guide. A person comparing “best indoor herb kits” may be evaluating options. A search for “buy indoor herb kit” may be better served by a product or category page.

These examples illustrate different needs, not measured search demand. Research helps decide which needs fit your offering and whether you already have a suitable page. Closely related searches do not automatically require separate pages.

A roadmap your content team can use

  • A prioritized topic list with the intended audience and search intent.
  • A page map showing where each topic belongs and which pages need review.
  • Suggested content angles, key questions, and supporting subtopics.
  • Notes on relevance, business value, available evidence, and uncertainty.

Priorities balance fit, effort, and opportunity. A term with fewer estimated searches can still be worth considering when it describes a service you actually provide and a clear customer need.

Before the research begins

Prepare your website URL, target market, main services, and a few competitors your customers would realistically compare with you. Include common customer questions and any subjects that fall outside your business. State whether the project needs local or multilingual research.

Will you create one page for every keyword?

No. Page decisions follow distinct user needs and content purpose. Similar terms can often be addressed together.

Does Zikap show live search volume on this website?

No. The public tool is an HTML checker. Any data-provider research must be agreed within the project scope.
