HOW IT WORKS

Clear scope.
Visible progress.

A useful project makes the problem, the work, and the next decision easy to understand.

ON THIS PAGE01 / Define the problem02 / Agree on the work03 / Review the evidence04 / Implement and verify05 / Monitor outcomes

01 / Define the problem

Start with the website URL, target audience, important pages, and what you want to improve. Note recent changes, such as a launch, redesign, or content migration. Your brief should describe the business need rather than just a desired ranking.

Create a customer account or sign in to request a package. Your dashboard holds your projects, acceptance updates, reports and scoped revision requests. Earlier requests can be linked once using the original private receipt.

02 / Agree on the work

Choose the service, the pages or topics included, the inputs required, and who is responsible for implementation. Agree the price, timeline, and output format before starting. Any exclusions or access limitations should be explicit.

A new task outside that scope should be reviewed before it is added, so priorities and expectations stay clear.

03 / Review the evidence

Findings should connect observations to affected pages and explain why an action is recommended. An automated warning is a starting point for investigation, not proof that a page will fail in search.

The output should distinguish urgent problems, worthwhile improvements, and optional cleanup. Data estimates should retain their provider, market, and date where relevant.

04 / Implement and verify

The agreed owner applies the changes. A follow-up review checks whether the intended changes are present and whether related pages still behave as expected. Keep a change log so later results can be interpreted in context.

05 / Monitor outcomes

Choose measures that match the project goal, such as resolved access issues, improved page clarity, or relevant queries and clicks in owner-provided Search Console reports. A local checker score is not a substitute for business outcomes.

Search changes can take time to appear, and results depend on factors outside a single project. No fixed ranking, traffic, or revenue outcome is promised.
